Why I speak to women about self-belief

I married young, became a mother early and did not finish college. When I realised my marriage might not work, I started working towards earning my own money. That gave me choices I would not otherwise have had.

I have had to rebuild more than once. I separated after 18 years of marriage. Both my parents had cancer at the same time and I looked after them. My father died while my mother was in treatment. I also had cancer myself. I know what it is like to look at your life and ask, “What do I do now?” That question is why I speak. You can read the full story on my About page.

What I speak about

  1. 01

    Believing in yourself again

    Confidence can drop after one hard event or fade slowly over years. I talk about how self-belief goes missing and what a first step back can look like.

  2. 02

    Having an identity of your own

    Family and work take most of the day. I talk about keeping a few interests that belong only to you.

  3. 03

    Building independence

    Earning my own money gave me the freedom to make my own decisions. I share what that looked like for me.

  4. 04

    Living through uncertainty

    Caring for my parents, my own cancer, starting over when plans changed. I talk about what that felt like and the practical things I did next.

  5. 05

    Finding enjoyment again

    Singing helped me through treatment. I talk about making room for music, laughter and ordinary pleasures when life is hard.

I kept singing through chemotherapy

When I was diagnosed I was living alone. The first thing I did was call my insurance agent to find out what my policy covered, and I began arranging my treatment before I told my family. That is how I cope: when something goes wrong, I start doing what needs to be done. Other people cope in other ways, and that is fine.

I carried on with my online music classes and kept singing through chemotherapy. Singing gave my days something that had nothing to do with being a patient. + one short, light line in Nandita’s words

Questions organisers ask

Nandita Thakur is a motivational speaker for women, an entrepreneur and the author of Silent Screams. She gives keynotes and motivational talks about rebuilding belief in yourself after life’s setbacks, drawing on her own experience of ending a long marriage, caring for her parents through cancer and being diagnosed with cancer herself.

She speaks on five themes: believing in yourself again, having an identity of your own, building independence, living through uncertainty and finding enjoyment again. Each comes from her own experience, told honestly, with questions the audience can take away.

Women’s communities, associations, event teams and people organising events for women inside companies. She also speaks to teams and middle managers about self-belief and keeping a life of your own outside work.

A few questions to think about, such as which decision they could start making for themselves and what next step feels manageable. Nandita shares her own experience. She is not a therapist or a financial adviser, and her talks do not promise a particular result.
